# Jira: Backlog, Sprints, and Releases

The **backlog** is the ordered list of all work the team has not yet finished. **Sprints** are the time-boxed sets of work the team commits to completing together. **Releases** are the versions of the product that work is shipped in. Together, they show what is waiting, what is being worked on now, and what is going out, and when.

## The Backlog

The backlog holds every issue in the project that is not yet part of a sprint or is waiting to be scheduled. It is ordered by priority, with the most important work at the top. Keeping the backlog ordered is the main job of whoever sets priorities for the team, often called the product owner in Scrum.

A healthy backlog is groomed regularly. Old items get closed, unclear items get clarified, and the top items are detailed enough to start soon. A backlog that grows without review becomes noise.

## Sprints

A sprint is a fixed period, typically one to four weeks, during which the team works on an agreed set of issues. Its main parts are:

- **Sprint planning:** the team pulls issues from the top of the backlog into the sprint and agrees on a goal.
- **Active sprint:** the issues in progress during the time-box. The sprint board shows its progress.
- **Sprint completion:** at the end, unfinished issues move back to the backlog or into the next sprint, and the team reviews what was completed.

Only one sprint is usually active at a time per board. Changing the scope of an active sprint should be rare, because it breaks the commitment the team made.

## Releases

A release, sometimes called a version, is a named milestone that groups issues delivered together, such as "1.4.0" or "Spring launch." Assigning issues to a release answers the question "what is in this version?" and makes release notes straightforward to produce.

Releases are independent of sprints. A release can span several sprints, and a sprint can contribute to more than one release.

## Key Idea

The backlog holds everything waiting, sprints hold what the team has committed to now, and releases hold what goes out together. Keeping the backlog ordered and each sprint focused is what makes planning predictable.
