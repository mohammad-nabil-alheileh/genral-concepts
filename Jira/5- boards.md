# Jira: Boards (Scrum and Kanban)

A **board** is the visual view of a project's issues. It shows work as cards arranged in columns, where each column corresponds to one or more statuses. Boards are how teams see what is happening, what is blocked, and what needs attention next.

## What a Board Shows

- **Columns** represent stages of the workflow, such as To Do, In Progress, and Done.
- **Cards** represent issues. Each card shows the key, summary, assignee, priority, and any labels or flags you configure.
- **Swimlanes** group cards into rows, for example by assignee, epic, or priority. They help when many issues are in progress at once.
- **Quick filters** narrow the board to a subset of cards, such as "my issues" or "high priority."

Boards do not hold issues themselves. They display issues from the project, so changing a card on the board changes the issue.

## Scrum Boards

A Scrum board is built around **sprints**, which are fixed time-boxes for completing a set of work. The board shows the current sprint's issues, and the backlog holds the work not yet planned into a sprint.

Scrum boards suit teams that plan in regular cycles and want to commit to a goal for each sprint. The sprint's start and end dates, and its progress, are visible on the board.

## Kanban Boards

A Kanban board shows a continuous flow of work with no fixed sprints. Issues move across columns as work progresses, and there is no sprint commitment to finish by a date.

Kanban boards often use **work-in-progress (WIP) limits**, which cap how many issues a column can hold at once. Limits make bottlenecks visible, because a full column signals that the team should finish existing work before starting more.

## Choosing Between Them

- Use **Scrum** when the team works in regular cycles and benefits from committing to a sprint goal.
- Use **Kanban** when work arrives unpredictably, such as support requests or maintenance, and the team needs to manage flow rather than plan in batches.

## Key Idea

A board is a live view of the workflow, not a separate place to store work. Scrum organizes the board around sprints, while Kanban organizes it around flow and limits.
